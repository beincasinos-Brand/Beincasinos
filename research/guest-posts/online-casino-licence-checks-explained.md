# How to Verify an Online Casino Licence Before You Register

*By BeInCasinos | Consumer education*

An online casino's homepage may display a licence badge, a company name and confident claims about regulation. Those details are a starting point, not proof on their own. A careful check means identifying the legal operator, finding the relevant regulator's own record and confirming that the record matches the website you intend to use.

This guide explains a practical verification process. Gambling laws vary by country, and a licence that applies in one jurisdiction does not necessarily permit an operator to accept customers elsewhere. This is general information, not legal advice or a recommendation to gamble.

## 1. Start with the law where you live

Before assessing a brand, establish whether the relevant online gambling product is lawful and available to you in your location. Use official government or regulator resources rather than relying only on an operator's marketing page or a comparison article.

Do not assume that an overseas licence gives a site permission to target your country. If local rules are unclear, pause and consult the relevant official source.

## 2. Find the legal operator

A casino's public-facing brand can differ from the company named in its terms. Look for the operator's full legal name, registered address, licence number and any listed trading names. Record the exact website domain too.

These details matter because similar brand names can be used by different businesses, and domains can change. If the terms name one company while the licence badge names another, seek an explanation and verify both against official records.

## 3. Use the regulator's own register

Search the relevant regulator's official register using the legal entity, trading name or domain where those search fields are available. In Great Britain, for example, the [UK Gambling Commission public register](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/public-register/businesses/) lets users check gambling businesses and their licence information.

Compare the record with the details on the website. Check the status, authorised activities and any relevant limitations or regulatory notices. Avoid treating a screenshot, logo or certificate uploaded by the operator as a substitute for the regulator's current record.

## 4. Check the scope—not just the existence—of a licence

A record may show that a business holds a licence, but that alone does not answer every question. The authorisation may cover specific activities, legal entities or territories. Confirm that the operator and product in question fit the permission described by the regulator.

If a site offers casino games, sports betting and other products, do not assume every product is covered by the same permission. Check the relevant details for the service you are considering.

## 5. Watch for warning signs

Pause if the operator's legal name is missing, the licence number cannot be found, the domain does not match the official record, or the site makes vague claims such as “licensed worldwide.” Broken regulator links, copied badges and conflicting company information also deserve scrutiny.

A missing result does not automatically prove fraud: registers differ and data may be displayed under a parent company or trading name. But uncertainty is a reason to investigate further—not a reason to trust the claim without evidence.

## 6. Record what you verified

For a transparent review, note the regulator consulted, the date of the check, the entity and domain searched, and what the record actually confirms. Link directly to the regulator's source when possible. If something cannot be verified, label it as unverified instead of guessing.

## A quick checklist

- Have I checked the rules that apply where I live?
- Did I identify the legal operator behind the brand?
- Does the regulator's own record match the entity and domain?
- Does the authorisation cover the relevant product and market?
- Have I recorded the source and date of the check?
- Are any important details still uncertain?

If you cannot establish the key facts, the prudent choice is to stop and seek reliable clarification before sharing personal information or making a deposit.
