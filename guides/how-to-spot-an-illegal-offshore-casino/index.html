How to Spot an Illegal Offshore Casino

An unlicensed casino can look as polished as a licensed one. These checks take five minutes and can save you from losing money with no recourse.

Written and reviewed by Anass Amir · Last reviewed 2026-10-30

Written and reviewed by Anass Amir · Last reviewed 2026-10-30

Check the licence first

Find the licence number and the company name in the terms and footer, then look them up on the regulator's own register for your country. If you cannot find the company, the domain or the product, treat the site as unlicensed.

Warning signs

  • A licence that does not cover your country.
  • Company names that differ between footer, terms and payment page.
  • Promises of “no verification”, “no limits” or “anonymous play”.
  • Only crypto or unusual payment methods.
  • No self-exclusion, limits or complaint route.
  • Very large bonuses with vague terms.

Why it matters

Unlicensed sites are outside the system that protects you: you cannot use national self-exclusion registers, complaints go nowhere and withdrawals can be blocked.

What to do instead

Use regulator lists: the UK Gambling Commission register, the Dutch KSA register, the German GGL whitelist, the Swiss ESBK list or the Michigan Gaming Control Board list. In Australia, online casino games are generally prohibited, so use only licensed wagering where legal.

If you already deposited

Keep all records, stop depositing, and contact your bank about your options. Report the site to your regulator and seek support if gambling has become hard to control.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Curaçao or Malta licence enough?

It depends on the country. If the market requires its own licence, a foreign licence does not replace it.

Are casino review sites a reliable check?

Only the regulator's register is reliable. Reviews should link to it.

Can I get my money back from an unlicensed site?

It is often impossible. Contact your bank and your regulator.
