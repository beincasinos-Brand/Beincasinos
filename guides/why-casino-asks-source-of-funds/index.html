Why a Casino Asks for Source of Funds, and What to Send

A source-of-funds request feels intrusive, but it is part of the anti-money-laundering rules licensed operators must follow.

Written and reviewed by Anass Amir · Last reviewed 2026-10-30

Written and reviewed by Anass Amir · Last reviewed 2026-10-30

Why operators ask

Licensed operators must take steps to prevent money laundering and to check that gambling is affordable. They may ask for evidence when deposits or withdrawals are large, unusual or inconsistent with what they know about you.

What counts as evidence

  • Recent payslips or an employment contract.
  • Bank statements showing the origin of the money.
  • Evidence of a sale, an inheritance or savings.
  • Documents that match the name on your casino account.

How to respond safely

Use only the secure upload in your account or the official support channel. Do not send documents through chat apps or to unknown addresses.

Hide information that is not needed, such as unrelated transactions, if the operator allows it, and send clear full-page images.

If a request seems excessive

Ask which rule requires the document and what it must show. A reasonable operator will explain. If the request seems unjustified, complain to the operator and then use the dispute route named in its terms.

Avoid the problem

Complete identity verification early, use payment methods in your own name and keep deposits consistent with your income.

Frequently asked questions

Is source of funds the same as KYC?

No. KYC confirms who you are; source of funds asks where the money came from.

Can the casino freeze my account?

Operators may restrict an account while checks are incomplete; ask what is needed and keep a written record.

What if I do not have documents?

Say so and ask what alternatives are acceptable.
