House edge explained: meaning, maths and limits

House edge is a theoretical measure of the mathematical advantage built into a game under stated rules. It is not a forecast of what any one player will lose.

Key point: a theoretical house edge describes an average across a very large number of plays under specific assumptions. It cannot predict a short session or guarantee a result.

What does house edge mean?

House edge is commonly used to describe the theoretical share of stakes that a game retains on average over time. The exact definition depends on the game and the calculation method, so check the rules and the source behind any published percentage. It is not a fee charged identically on every individual play.

How is it related to RTP?

Return to player (RTP) expresses a game's theoretical prize return as a percentage of stakes. Where RTP and house edge use the same stake base, rules, game configuration and assumptions, the theoretical house edge is the complement of RTP:

Theoretical house edge (%) = 100% − theoretical RTP (%)

For a hypothetical game with a stated theoretical RTP of 96%, that arithmetic gives a 4% theoretical house edge. This example explains the calculation only; it is not a claim about any particular game, operator or market.

Why this does not predict personal losses

The theoretical average applies across a large number of plays, not as a fixed deduction from every stake. Short-term results can differ substantially: a person might lose more or less than the theoretical average, and any individual result remains uncertain. A theoretical edge is not a promise that the operator will retain exactly that share over a particular day, player or session.

House edge, RTP and probability are different

  • RTP: theoretical prize return as a proportion of stakes over a large number of plays.
  • House edge: under matching assumptions, the complementary theoretical advantage.
  • Probability: the chance of a specified event, such as a particular result occurring.
  • Volatility: how widely outcomes can vary around an average.

One number cannot substitute for the others. A game with a particular RTP does not reveal the chance of a specific prize or the size and frequency of every outcome.

What should you check before trusting a figure?

  1. Confirm the exact game, version and configuration the figure refers to.
  2. Read the game rules and see whether the figure is theoretical or based on observed results.
  3. Check whether the calculation uses the same stake definition and prize rules as the comparison figure.
  4. Look for a named, accountable source and a date where relevant.
  5. Do not treat the percentage as a prediction, income estimate or reason to increase stakes.

Primary-source reading

The UK Gambling Commission's guide to return to player explains RTP as an average over many plays and why it should not be expected in every session. Its guidance is specific to the regulator's remit; rules and disclosures can differ across games and jurisdictions.

Frequently asked questions

Does a 4% house edge mean I lose 4% of every stake?

No. It is a theoretical average under stated assumptions, not a fixed charge on every play or a personal forecast.

Can house edge show whether I will win?

No. It does not predict the next outcome, a short session, or an individual result.

Does a lower house edge make gambling risk-free?

No. Gambling can still result in losing money. A mathematical average is not a guarantee of safety, profit or suitability for an individual's circumstances.
