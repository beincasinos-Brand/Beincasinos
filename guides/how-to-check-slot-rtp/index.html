How to Check a Slot's RTP Before You Play

Return to player is a long-run theoretical average. Some slots are offered in more than one RTP version, so check the version at your casino.

Where to find it

Open the game's information or help screen. It normally lists the RTP and the rules.

Versions

Providers can supply several RTP settings, and an operator chooses one. The same title can therefore differ between sites.

What the number means

96% does not mean you receive 96 for every 100 staked in a session. It describes the long-run average over a very large number of plays.

Volatility

Volatility describes how uneven the results are. Two slots with the same RTP can feel very different.

Use it carefully

RTP helps compare games; it does not predict your result. Set limits regardless.

Where to find the figure

Most slots show the theoretical return-to-player in the game's information or help screen. Some games publish several RTP settings, and an operator may choose one, so the number on the screen of the casino you are using is the one that matters.

If you cannot find the RTP, that is itself information. Ask support or choose a game where it is shown.

What RTP does and does not mean

RTP is a long-run theoretical average across a very large number of spins. It does not predict a single session, and it does not mean you will get back that share of your own stake.

A game with a higher RTP costs less on average, but volatility decides how bumpy the ride is. Pick a stake you can afford, set a limit, and treat every spin as entertainment, not a way to make money.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I find a slot's RTP?

In the game information screen, not usually on the lobby tile.

Is a higher RTP better?

In theory it loses less over the long run, but results in short sessions vary widely.
