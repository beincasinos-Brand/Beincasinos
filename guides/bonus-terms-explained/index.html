Bonus terms, explained

Promotional headlines leave out important details. Use this guide to identify the conditions that determine what an offer actually means.

Remember: a bonus is not free money. It can require deposits, wagering, identity checks, and acceptance of restrictions. You can always decline it.

Start with the full terms

Read the complete offer rules before opting in or depositing. Save a copy with the date, because terms can change. If a condition is unclear, ask the operator for a written explanation before proceeding.

Wagering multiplier

A wagering requirement states how much eligible wagering is required before a bonus or associated winnings may become withdrawable. Check whether the multiplier applies to the bonus, the deposit, or both.

Great Britain: check the current bonus rules

For operators and incentives within the relevant Great Britain licensing framework, the UK Gambling Commission’s Social Responsibility Code 5.1.1 took effect in its updated form on 19 January 2026. It requires incentive terms to be clear, transparent, fair and readily accessible. It also prohibits wagering requirements that require a customer to play through bonus funds more than 10 times, and prohibits combining more than one type of gambling product in one incentive. These are Great Britain rules, not a worldwide standard; check the operator, product and jurisdiction before applying them.

The 10× limit does not mean an offer is good value or that winnings are guaranteed. The calculation base, eligible games, maximum stake, expiry, cashout limits and treatment of deposited cash still matter. Read the official Licence Condition 5.1.1 and the complete offer terms. Our bonus expiry guide covers deadlines and what to record.

Eligible games and contribution

Some games may count differently toward wagering or may be excluded entirely. Check the eligible-game list, contribution percentages, maximum bet rules, and any prohibited strategies.

Work out the requirement before opting in

Use the exact base named in the terms. For example, a hypothetical 20-unit bonus at 30× bonus wagering means 600 units of eligible wagering (20 × 30). If the terms instead say 10× deposit plus bonus on a 20-unit deposit and a 20-unit bonus, the arithmetic base may be 40 units, making 400 units at 10×. These are examples only: never assume the base from a headline, and check whether the actual terms and local rules permit the condition.

Wagering turnover is the amount staked across eligible plays, not a target profit and not a promise of a return. If only some games contribute, the amount of real activity needed can differ from the headline multiplier. A contribution of 20% means a 100-unit stake adds 20 units to the qualifying turnover, if that game and stake are eligible under the terms.

Separate bonus rules from cash-withdrawal rules

Do not treat these as the same thing: a wagering requirement, a maximum-bet rule while a bonus is active, a maximum cashout, a withdrawal limit, and an identity or anti-money-laundering check. Each can have a different trigger and consequence. Save the exact wording for each and ask the operator to identify the clause it relies on if a balance is restricted.

Expiry and cashout limits

Look for the deadline to opt in, the period to complete wagering, maximum cashout clauses, and what happens to unused bonus funds when a deadline passes. Our detailed guide explains bonus expiry dates, activation windows, and what to check when a deadline passes.

Some offers also define a maximum cashout or winnings cap. Read our guide to maximum cashout clauses to check which balance the cap applies to and what remains unclear.

Other restrictions to find

  • Minimum deposit and payment methods
  • Country or account eligibility
  • Maximum stake while a bonus is active
  • Whether withdrawals cancel the bonus
  • Identity verification and withdrawal requirements
  • Whether promotional funds can be converted or withdrawn

For a worked arithmetic example, use the wagering requirement calculator. Enter only the amount and multiplier specified by the terms; the tool cannot determine which games count, whether a promotion is lawful for you, or whether any balance will be withdrawable.

A simple example

If an offer’s terms state that a 20-unit bonus has a 10× bonus wagering requirement, the stated requirement would be 200 units of eligible wagering (20 × 10). This is an arithmetic illustration, not a real offer, and it does not imply that the player will win or recover that amount.

Check the headline against the significant terms

Do not judge an offer by the headline alone. Before opting in, locate the eligibility rules, minimum deposit, wagering base and multiplier, eligible games, maximum stake, expiry deadline, maximum cashout, and what happens after a withdrawal or cancellation. If a material condition is hard to find or conflicts with the headline, save a copy and ask the operator to clarify it in writing before accepting.

For Great Britain, compare the offer with the Gambling Commission’s consumer guide to free offers and bonuses. The UK advertising regulator’s guidance on gambling free bets and bonuses also explains why significant conditions should be clear and prominent. These sources describe the relevant British framework; local rules differ elsewhere.

Decision checklist

Can you explain the requirement in your own words? Do you know the time limit, eligible games, stake limits, and withdrawal rules? If not, skip the offer rather than relying on the headline.
