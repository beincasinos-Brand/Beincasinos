ACMA Blocked Gambling Sites: What the Block Means

Blocking is one tool ACMA uses against illegal interactive gambling services. It reduces access, but a site that is not blocked is not therefore legal.

Written and reviewed by Anass Amir · Last reviewed 2026-10-30

Written and reviewed by Anass Amir · Last reviewed 2026-10-30

What ACMA does

ACMA investigates illegal interactive gambling services and can ask Australian internet service providers to block access to them. It publishes information about enforcement and blocked services on its website.

Why blocked sites reappear

Operators may launch new domains. A site that opens is not evidence of legality, and a new domain may simply not have been blocked yet.

What it means for you

Using a blocked or unlicensed site means you are outside Australian protections. Deposits may be hard to recover and BetStop will not cover you.

Check before you bet

For wagering, use ACMA's official check page and register to match the trading name, the licence holder and the exact URL. For casino games, remember they are generally prohibited from being provided to people in Australia.

Support

If gambling is a problem, Gambling Help Online offers free and confidential help on 1800 858 858.

Frequently asked questions

Does ACMA block every illegal site?

No. New domains appear, so absence of a block is not proof of legality.

Is using a VPN legal?

The Act targets providers, but using a VPN to reach a prohibited service leaves you without protection.

Where can I check a wagering licence?

Use ACMA's official pages and the state or territory regulator.
