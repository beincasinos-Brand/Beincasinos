How to Spot an Untrustworthy Casino Review Site

Most casino review sites earn money when you sign up. That is not wrong in itself, but it makes it important to know how to tell honest reviews from adverts.

Why this matters

Casino review sites are often paid affiliates, which means their income depends on you clicking through. A trustworthy site discloses this and shows its method; an untrustworthy site hides it.

Warning signs

  • Every casino scores almost the same, and the highest scores go to the biggest bonuses.
  • No dates, so you cannot tell when anything was checked.
  • No explanation of how scores are produced.
  • Licence claims with no register link you can check yourself.
  • “Exclusive” bonuses that you cannot find on the operator's own site.
  • No contact details, no named publisher, no corrections policy.

What a good review shows

A good review names the legal operator and the exact domain, links to the regulator's register, quotes the bonus terms with a date, and says clearly what it could not verify.

It also explains how the site earns money and states that payment does not change scores.

Test it yourself

Pick one claim on the page, such as the licence, and verify it on the regulator's own register. Then open the operator's bonus page and compare the terms with the review. If either does not match, treat the review with caution.

How we try to meet this standard

Our scores follow a published method, show evidence dates and leave items unrated when we cannot verify them. Read how we score and our affiliate disclosure and judge for yourself.

Frequently asked questions

Are affiliate review sites biased?

They can be. Look for disclosure, a published method and verifiable evidence rather than assuming either way.

How do I know a bonus on a review site is real?

Open the operator's own page and compare the terms and the date.

Should I trust awards on review sites?

Only if the criteria and the evidence are published.
