Signs of Problem Gambling and Where to Get Help

Gambling is entertainment for many people, but it can become harmful. Knowing the signs early makes it easier to act.

Common warning signs

  • Spending more money or time than you planned.
  • Chasing losses with bigger bets.
  • Borrowing or using money needed for bills.
  • Hiding or lying about how much you gamble.
  • Feeling irritable, anxious or low when you are not gambling.
  • Gambling to escape stress or problems.

A quick self-check

Ask yourself honestly whether you gamble more than you can afford, whether you have tried to cut down and failed, and whether gambling affects your relationships, work or mood. Recognised screening tools are available from the support organisations below.

Steps you can take now

  • Set deposit limits and time-outs, or exclude yourself for a longer period.
  • Remove saved cards and block gambling sites and apps on your devices.
  • Tell someone you trust.
  • Speak to a support service or your doctor.

Where to get help

In Great Britain, the National Gambling Helpline and BeGambleAware offer free confidential support. GAMSTOP lets you exclude yourself from licensed online operators. In other countries, check your regulator's site or local health service for the right contact.

Supporting someone else

Be calm and non-judgemental. Encourage them to talk to a professional, and look after your own wellbeing, too. Support is available for family members as well.

Frequently asked questions

Is problem gambling an addiction?

It can be. Gambling disorder is recognised as a health condition, and treatment is available.

Is the help confidential?

Helplines are generally confidential and free; check each service's own policy.

Can I recover?

Many people do, and early support makes it easier.
