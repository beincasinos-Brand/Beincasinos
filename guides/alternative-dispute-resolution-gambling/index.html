Alternative dispute resolution for gambling complaints

Alternative dispute resolution (ADR) is a process for resolving some disputes without starting court proceedings. Whether it is available, who can handle the case, and what the decision means depend on the jurisdiction, operator, product, and dispute.

Scope: This guide explains general concepts and uses Great Britain as a specific example. It is not legal advice, does not determine whether a particular operator is authorised, and cannot promise a refund or a favourable outcome. Rules and provider arrangements can change.

What ADR can—and cannot—do

An ADR provider is an independent body that considers a dispute within its scheme rules. Depending on the scheme, it may assess documents, ask the parties for information, facilitate settlement, or issue an adjudication. Check the provider's remit and whether an outcome is binding before relying on it.

ADR is not the same as reporting an operator to a regulator. A regulator may investigate licensing or conduct without deciding an individual's compensation claim. Likewise, a public review or social-media post does not replace a formal complaint route.

Start with the operator's complaint process

Find the current complaints procedure in the operator's terms or official website. Submit a concise, factual account through the stated channel and keep a copy of the complaint, attachments, submission date, reference number, and response. Ask for the final decision and the relevant term or rule if the answer is unclear.

Do not assume every complaint qualifies for ADR. Some schemes cover only certain transaction outcomes, operator entities, products, or locations. Customer-service dissatisfaction alone may be outside a scheme's remit.

Great Britain: verify the current route

For gambling businesses licensed by the UK Gambling Commission, the Commission's current consumer guidance says a customer must first complete the business's own complaints procedure. If the issue remains unresolved after eight weeks, the customer may be able to take the complaint to an ADR provider. The provider decides whether the dispute falls within its remit; some types of complaint, including customer-service issues, are excluded. Check the current official guidance and the operator's procedure for the exact conditions.

Regulatory arrangements changed in 2026. The Gambling Commission states that from April 2026 it is no longer the competent authority for approving and monitoring ADR providers under the new requirements, with the Chartered Trading Standards Institute taking that role. Therefore, do not rely on old articles or a copied list alone: confirm the current competent authority, provider status, and scheme eligibility.

Prepare a clear evidence pack

  • A dated timeline separating direct observations from assumptions.
  • The exact domain, operator's legal name, relevant product, and country or state, where verified.
  • The terms that applied at the time, including relevant bonus or withdrawal wording.
  • Transaction references, account messages, complaint correspondence, and the operator's final response.
  • A short description of the outcome you are asking for and why you believe the evidence supports it.

Share only what the provider needs. Redact passwords, one-time codes, full payment-card details, unrelated identity information, and other people's personal data. Use the provider's independently verified official submission route.

Check deadlines and the effect of a decision

Look for the deadline to complain, the date from which it is calculated, any eligibility exclusions, whether the provider can refuse the case, and whether a decision is binding on either party. Preserve the original records and do not assume that contacting a regulator pauses a court or scheme deadline. If the amount or legal issue is significant, seek qualified advice in the relevant jurisdiction.

Watch for recovery scams

Be suspicious of anyone who guarantees a refund, demands an upfront recovery fee, requests remote access to your device, or asks for your password, seed phrase, or one-time code. Independently verify any organisation that claims to be a regulator, ADR provider, lawyer, or recovery specialist.

If a payment may be unauthorised or fraudulent, contact your bank or payment provider through its official channel and explain the facts accurately. A chargeback or ADR process does not guarantee that funds will be recovered.

Practical next steps

  1. Identify the legal operator and jurisdiction; do not infer them from a brand name alone.
  2. Use the operator's formal complaint process and retain proof of submission.
  3. Read the relevant regulator's current consumer guidance and verify the ADR provider independently.
  4. Check scheme scope, deadlines, evidence requirements, fees, and whether the outcome is binding.
  5. If no ADR route applies, ask the relevant regulator or consumer-protection authority what other official options exist; consider qualified local advice where appropriate.
