Probability vs RTP: what is the difference?

These terms describe different parts of a game's mathematics. Knowing the distinction helps readers avoid over-interpreting a percentage.

Educational note: This guide explains mathematical concepts only. It is not a strategy, a prediction, or an encouragement to gamble.

Probability describes the chance of an event

Probability describes how likely a defined event is under a stated model. It is usually expressed from 0 to 1 or as a percentage. The event must be defined clearly: for example, the chance of a particular outcome on one fair die roll is different from the chance of that outcome at least once across many rolls.

RTP describes a theoretical average return

Return to player (RTP) is generally a theoretical long-run measure of the proportion of qualifying stakes a game is designed to return under its specified rules. It does not tell you the probability of a particular symbol, prize, or result. Different outcome distributions can share the same theoretical RTP.

A simple non-gambling example

Imagine a fictional prize draw with 100 equally likely tickets and one ticket that wins 50 points. The chance that one ticket wins is 1 in 100, or 1%. The expected prize value per ticket is 0.5 points before considering any ticket price. Probability describes the chance of the event; expected value describes an average amount under the model. RTP is a game-specific term and should not be casually substituted for either one.

Why RTP does not reveal the next outcome

A long-run average is not a schedule of returns. Outcomes in a real game can vary widely around the theoretical average, and the published figure alone does not specify how often a particular event occurs or how large a possible win may be. Past results do not, by themselves, establish what the next independent random outcome will be.

Where volatility fits

Volatility (sometimes called variance in general discussion, though the precise technical measure can differ) concerns how spread out outcomes are. It is not the same as RTP. Two hypothetical games can have similar theoretical RTP while producing very different patterns of smaller and larger outcomes. Check the provider's exact definition rather than assuming a label is standardised across all products.

Questions to ask about a percentage

  • What exact event, game version and rules does the figure describe?
  • Is it a probability of one outcome, a theoretical return, or a measure of variability?
  • Who published it, and is there traceable technical or regulatory evidence?
  • Does the explanation distinguish long-run averages from short-term outcomes?
  • Are assumptions, uncertainty and product-specific limitations stated clearly?
