Cruks Explained: Self-Exclusion for Online Gambling in the Netherlands

Cruks is the Centraal Register Uitsluiting Kansspelen. Registered people are excluded from online gambling with licensed Dutch operators and from many land-based venues.

How registration works

You can register yourself, and in some cases a third party or a court can request registration. Licensed operators must check Cruks before letting you play.

Periods

Exclusion periods start at six months and can be much longer.

What it does not cover

It covers the regulated Dutch market. Unlicensed sites are outside it, which is why checking the licence matters.

Support

If you want help, Dutch addiction care and helplines are available; ask your GP or find them via the KSA.

How the register works in practice

Cruks is the central register for exclusion from gambling in the Netherlands. Providers with a Dutch licence must check it before letting someone play, so one registration covers all licensed online providers, not just one site.

You can register yourself or, in some circumstances, be registered by others under the rules set out by the regulator. The minimum period and the way to end a registration are set by Dutch law, so read the current rules on the official Cruks page before you register.

Why only licensed sites protect you

The register works only where operators are obliged to use it. A site without a Dutch licence has no duty to check Cruks, so exclusion does not protect you there. Check the Kansspelautoriteit licence list before you deposit anywhere.

If you need support, speak to your GP or an addiction-care service, and use the self-test tools the regulator links to.

Frequently asked questions

What is Cruks?

The Dutch central register to exclude yourself from gambling.

Do licensed operators check it?

Yes, they must.
