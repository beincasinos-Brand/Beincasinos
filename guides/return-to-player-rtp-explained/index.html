Return to player (RTP), explained

RTP is a long-run statistical measure, not a promise about what one person will win. This guide explains the term, the arithmetic, and its limits.

Key point: a displayed RTP is not a forecast for one session, a guarantee of payout, or a way to make gambling profitable.

What does RTP mean?

Return to player (RTP) is a theoretical percentage of total stakes that a game is designed to return as prizes over a very large number of plays. If a game has a theoretical RTP of 96%, the mathematical complement is a 4% theoretical house edge under the same assumptions. Neither figure predicts the outcome of a short session.

A simple calculation

RTP can be expressed as total prizes divided by total stakes, multiplied by 100. For illustration, if a game records 96,000 units in prizes from 100,000 units of total stakes over a sufficiently large sample, its observed RTP for that sample is 96%. Actual results can differ from the theoretical target, especially over smaller samples.

Why the average does not apply to every player

RTP is an aggregate measure. A single player may receive no prizes, a small amount, or a large prize; the long-run percentage does not say what will happen in any particular sequence. It also does not mean that a player gets a fixed percentage of each stake back.

For random games, previous wins and losses do not make a future outcome “due.” A losing streak does not itself prove that the next result is more likely to win.

RTP is not the same as probability of winning

RTP describes the total value of prizes relative to stakes over time. It does not directly tell you how frequently any prize occurs, how large prizes are distributed, or the chance of a specific outcome. Those depend on the game’s rules and probability model.

What should you verify?

  • Read the game’s own rules and information screen.
  • Check whether the stated RTP applies to the exact game version and configuration.
  • Look for the source of the figure and whether it is theoretical or measured over a historical sample.
  • Do not use RTP to estimate personal returns, plan income, or justify chasing losses.

Official reference

The UK Gambling Commission’s RTP explainer describes RTP as an average over a significant number of plays and warns that it should not be expected in each session. Its specific rules concern the products and jurisdiction covered by that regulator; other markets may differ.

Frequently asked questions

Does 96% RTP mean I get 96 back for every 100 I stake?

No. It is a long-run theoretical average across many plays, not a guaranteed personal return.

Can a higher RTP guarantee a win?

No. RTP alone cannot guarantee a win, predict a particular outcome, or remove the risk of losing the amount staked.

Can I compare RTP values without checking anything else?

Not reliably. Confirm that the figures apply to the exact game versions and understand that RTP does not describe volatility or short-term outcomes.
