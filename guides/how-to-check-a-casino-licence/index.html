How to check a casino licence

A logo or licence number on a website is a claim—not proof. Learn how to check who holds the licence and what it covers.

Important: Licensing systems differ by country. This guide is general education, not legal advice. Always use the regulator responsible for your location and the specific product.

1. Identify the legal operator

Find the operator’s legal name, company number if provided, registered address, and licence details in the site’s terms or footer. A brand name can differ from the company that actually provides the service.

2. Open the regulator’s own register

Do not rely only on a badge, a search snippet, or a third-party directory. Navigate to the regulator’s official website yourself and search for the exact legal entity. For Great Britain, the Gambling Commission public register is a primary source. Other jurisdictions have different authorities and rules.

3. Match the details carefully

  • Does the register show the same legal entity, not merely a similar brand?
  • Is the licence active, and does its scope cover the relevant activity?
  • Does the official record list the website domain or trading name, where applicable?
  • Do the operator’s stated details match the regulator record?

4. Check the rules for your location

A licence in one jurisdiction does not automatically mean an operator is authorised to offer gambling in every country. Confirm local law and whether the regulator’s protections apply to you.

5. Treat uncertainty as a reason to stop

If the record cannot be found, the legal entity differs, or the operator pressures you to act before you can verify the claim, do not deposit money while the uncertainty remains. Consider seeking advice from the relevant regulator or consumer-protection authority.

Official source and limitations

The UK Gambling Commission business register can be searched by business name, trading name, domain, or account number. Its notes explain that some domain and trading-name details are supplied by businesses and may not be independently guaranteed. A register entry is evidence to interpret carefully—not universal approval for every country, product, or brand.

For any other market, locate the regulator responsible for that territory and confirm its scope and current records directly. If you cannot establish whether a service is authorised for your location, do not treat a badge or a foreign licence as sufficient proof.

Quick checklist

  • Legal operator identified
  • Official regulator register consulted
  • Licence status and scope checked
  • Domain/entity details matched
  • Local eligibility and dispute options understood
