European vs American Roulette: House Edge Compared

The difference between European and American roulette is a single pocket, and it nearly doubles what the game costs you.

The one-pocket difference

European roulette has 37 pockets: numbers 1 to 36 and one zero. American roulette has 38, because it adds a double zero.

What it does to the house edge

A straight-up bet pays 35 to 1 in both games. With 37 pockets the house edge is about 2.70%; with 38 it is about 5.26%. Over many bets, American roulette costs roughly twice as much per unit staked.

What the edge means in practice

A house edge is a long-run average. It does not predict what happens in one session, and no betting system changes it, because every spin is independent.

Choosing a table

  • Check the wheel type in the game name or information screen.
  • Look for “La Partage” or “En Prison” rules on French variants, which reduce the edge on even-money bets.
  • Check table limits so that your stakes fit your budget.

Stay in control

Roulette moves fast. Use reality checks and deposit limits, and do not raise stakes to recover losses.

Frequently asked questions

Which roulette has better odds?

European, because it has one zero instead of two.

Does the Martingale system work?

No. It cannot beat the house edge and exposes you to very large losses.

Is online roulette fair?

Licensed games are tested; the house edge is built into the rules.
