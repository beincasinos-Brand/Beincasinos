How to report and complain about an online casino

To report an online casino problem, document what happened, identify the legal operator and the regulator for the relevant market, then follow the operator’s formal complaints process before escalating. The correct route depends on the product, contract, and jurisdiction.

Important: This is general information, not legal advice or a promise that a complaint will recover money. Do not deposit more, gamble more, or pay a third party because they promise to fix the problem.

1. Preserve the evidence first

Save copies of the account terms that applied at the time, bonus terms if relevant, transaction history, payment confirmations, withdrawal status, account messages, support replies, and the exact dates. Keep the page URLs and screenshots where lawful. Do not alter the originals, share passwords, or send full payment-card details in an ordinary email.

Write a short timeline: what happened, when it happened, what the operator said, and what outcome you are asking for. Separate what you personally observed from what you suspect.

2. Identify the legal operator and the right market

Brand names and website domains do not always identify the legal entity. Check the operator's terms and the relevant regulator's official register. Match the exact domain, company name, product, and jurisdiction. A licence or complaint route in one country does not automatically apply elsewhere.

If the operator identity or permission cannot be verified, say that clearly in your records rather than assuming a regulator supervises the site.

3. Use the operator's formal complaint process

Find the complaints or dispute-resolution section in the current terms. Follow its required steps and deadlines, and keep a copy of the message and proof of submission. Include your timeline, relevant evidence, account reference only where needed, and a specific requested resolution. Avoid threats or claims you cannot support.

Ask the operator to explain its decision and identify the rule or term it relied on. Do not send unnecessary sensitive information. If the operator requests identity documents, verify the request through an official account or support channel first.

4. Check the independent escalation route

Use the regulator's official website for the relevant licence and market to confirm whether it accepts consumer complaints, supervises the product, or lists an approved alternative dispute-resolution body. Some regulators investigate licensing or conduct but do not decide individual compensation claims. An ADR provider may have its own eligibility rules and time limits.

Do not assume that a public complaint form, regulator report, or review-site post replaces the operator's formal process. Follow the exact official instructions for your jurisdiction and product.

Official starting points in selected markets

These links explain different processes; they are not interchangeable and none guarantees a refund. Confirm the current instructions and the exact operator/product before submitting personal information.

  • Great Britain: Gambling Commission — how to complain. Start by complaining to the gambling business using its formal procedure. The Commission says the business has up to 8 weeks to resolve the complaint. If it remains unresolved after that period, an eligible dispute may be referred to the relevant approved alternative dispute-resolution (ADR) provider; check the Commission’s current ADR guidance for eligibility and next steps. This timeframe is specific to the Great Britain process described by the Commission and should not be applied to other countries.
  • Australia: ACMA — interactive gambling complaint form. This is for reporting potentially prohibited interactive gambling services, not a guarantee that ACMA will recover an individual balance.
  • France: ANJ — resolving a dispute with an operator. Its mediation route applies to operators/categories within its stated remit; online casino games are not generally authorised in France. Reporting an illegal offer is different from seeking compensation.
  • Germany: GGL — report a violation. GGL says its reporting portal does not handle transaction disputes with a gambling provider; use the operator's process and seek the relevant independent route where available.
  • Netherlands: Kansspelautoriteit — contact and reporting. Check whether your issue belongs with the regulator or the operator; a regulatory report is not automatically a personal dispute-resolution process.
  • Switzerland: Federal Gaming Board — unauthorised online games. Use official guidance to report an unauthorised offering; this is not a promise of individual compensation.
  • United States: There is no single nationwide online-casino complaint route. Identify the state and product, then use the relevant state regulator's official website and the operator's contract/complaint process. Do not assume a licence in one state covers another.

5. Be careful with chargebacks and recovery offers

If a payment appears unauthorised or fraudulent, contact your bank or payment provider promptly using the contact details on its official website or card. Explain the facts accurately. A chargeback is not guaranteed, and you should not misrepresent a transaction or hide relevant details.

Be wary of anyone who guarantees a refund, asks for an upfront recovery fee, requests remote access to your device, or wants your password, wallet seed phrase, or one-time code. A person claiming to be a regulator or recovery specialist should be independently verified.

6. Keep the complaint proportionate and private

Send only the information required for the process. Redact unrelated account, identity, and payment details before sharing documents publicly. Do not publish another person's private information. Keep a log of submission dates, reference numbers, responses, and any stated deadline.

What a useful complaint record contains

  • Exact website domain and legal operator, if verified.
  • Country, product, and relevant dates.
  • Short, chronological description of events.
  • Relevant terms and official source links.
  • Copies of correspondence and transaction records, with unnecessary sensitive data removed.
  • The specific resolution requested and the formal route used.

When to stop and seek independent help

If the dispute involves a significant amount, suspected fraud, identity theft, or complex legal rights, consider advice from a qualified local adviser or the relevant consumer-protection authority. If gambling is affecting your wellbeing or finances, pause play and look for verified support and self-exclusion options in your country.
