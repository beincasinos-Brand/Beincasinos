Casino review evidence checklist

A repeatable method for checking a casino review before trusting its conclusion. Use dated sources, keep unknowns visible, and do not treat a score as a substitute for eligibility checks.

Start with the evidence, not the ranking. A polished page or a high score does not establish that a casino is licensed for your location, that its terms are suitable, or that the reviewer actually tested the service.

1. Match the brand, domain and legal operator

  • Write down the exact domain, including country-specific subdomains.
  • Find the legal entity named in the site's terms and footer.
  • Match that entity, trading name and domain against the relevant regulator's current register.
  • Check the licence permits the product being discussed and the territory where it is offered.
  • Record the date and link to every regulator page used. If the match is ambiguous, mark it unresolved rather than guessing.

Related reading: how to check a casino licence and brand name versus legal operator.

2. Read the actual terms behind every promotion

  • Capture the full offer terms and the date checked.
  • Record eligibility, qualifying deposit, wagering requirements, expiry, maximum stake, excluded games and withdrawal restrictions.
  • Check whether a bonus is optional and what happens if it is declined or cancelled.
  • Do not repeat a headline offer from a search result or affiliate page without confirming the operator's own current terms.

Related reading: bonus terms explained and wagering requirements.

3. Separate deposit, processing and withdrawal facts

Do not use “instant payouts” as a general claim without evidence. Record the payment method, any operator review or pending period, verification conditions, fees, limits and the separate time taken by the bank or wallet. A published estimate is not a guarantee for every account.

Related reading: withdrawal processing times and payment-method ownership checks.

4. Verify complaints and independent escalation

Find the operator's formal complaint steps and identify the named alternative dispute resolution (ADR) provider, if applicable. Record eligibility, escalation stages and deadlines from the official process. A help-centre page describing internal complaints does not, by itself, prove which independent route is available.

Related reading: how to complain about a casino and ADR in gambling disputes.

5. Check safer-gambling information and usability

  • Locate deposit limits, time-outs, self-exclusion and reality-check settings.
  • Check whether support and self-exclusion information is easy to find.
  • Verify any national exclusion-system claim against an official source for that market.
  • Do not claim that a tool works well unless its availability and user journey have actually been checked and documented.

This checklist is informational and is not an endorsement of gambling. If gambling is causing harm, consider using local support and self-exclusion resources rather than looking for another operator.

6. Read enforcement records in context

Search the regulator's licence, trading-name, domain, sanctions and news pages. Distinguish an allegation from an established finding, and a historic settlement from evidence about current controls. A blank account-specific action page does not prove that no historical action exists; regulators may publish records in separate places or apply retention periods.

For Great Britain, start with the UK Gambling Commission public register and the regulator's news and enforcement announcements.

7. Evaluate the review itself

  • Does each material claim have a primary source and check date?
  • Does the reviewer disclose affiliate commissions, paid placement and other commercial relationships?
  • Does the review distinguish hands-on tests from desk research and operator statements?
  • Are limitations, missing data and complaints-sample sizes explained?
  • Is the review specific to the right country, product and operator?
  • Is there a visible correction and update policy?

See how to evaluate a casino review site and our review methodology.

8. Use a clear pass / hold decision

Before scoring, confirm the identity and licence gates. If either fails or remains uncertain, hold the profile. If current terms, payments, complaints or safer-gambling evidence is missing, disclose the gap and do not fill it with assumptions. Ratings should follow evidence; they should never be used to disguise missing evidence.

Suggested research log: brand and domain · legal operator · market and product · regulator URL · date checked · terms URL and date · payment/withdrawal evidence · complaints/ADR evidence · safer-gambling controls · enforcement history · independent feedback sample · unresolved questions · second-editor sign-off.
