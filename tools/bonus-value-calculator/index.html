How the calculation works

Bonus = deposit × match %, limited by the cap. Turnover = (bonus or deposit + bonus) × wagering multiple. Theoretical cost = turnover × (1 − RTP). The average result is the bonus minus that cost. A positive figure means the bonus is worth more than the theoretical cost of clearing it; a negative figure means that on average it costs more than it gives.

Why it is only an average

RTP describes the long run over very large numbers of bets. In a single session you can finish far above or below the figure. Game contribution, maximum-bet rules, expiry, and a cap on withdrawable winnings are not modelled here, and they can change the outcome a lot.

What a negative result tells you

It does not mean the offer is a trap, only that the arithmetic is unfavourable at that RTP. Offers with a low multiple, a bonus-only base and a high-RTP eligible game score best. You can always decline a bonus.

Common questions

Is a casino bonus ever worth it?

Sometimes, when the multiple is low, it applies to the bonus only, the games you enjoy count in full and there is no tiny cashout cap. This calculator lets you test that with real numbers.

What RTP should I enter?

Use the RTP shown in the game's information screen. If you are unsure, enter a typical figure such as 96, and check the sensitivity by trying 94 and 98.

Does the calculator include the max cashout?

No. If the terms cap what you can withdraw from bonus winnings, the real value can be lower than shown.

Does a higher wagering multiple always mean a worse bonus?

Usually, because turnover rises in proportion. Compare offers by running each through the same calculation.
