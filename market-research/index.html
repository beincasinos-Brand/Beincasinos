Research framework

Check the market before you publish.

“International English” is a language strategy, not a legal permission. Eligibility and marketing rules must be reviewed market by market.

Market readiness checklist

  • Identify the target country and the audience’s age requirements.
  • Find the relevant regulator and read current official rules.
  • Verify whether the operator and its domain may legally serve that market.
  • Review the affiliate program’s allowed territories and promotional restrictions.
  • Check rules for bonuses, comparisons, testimonials, claims, and paid traffic.
  • Review privacy, cookies, tracking, and marketing-consent obligations.
  • Publish the real publisher identity and clear commercial disclosures.
  • Verify support resources and escalation routes for that country.
  • Document the source, date checked, reviewer, and next review date.

Primary-source starting points for the intended markets

  • Australia: ACMA — Interactive Gambling Act. Online casino services are generally prohibited under Australian law; do not promote offshore casino offers as locally legal or available.
  • United States: New York State Gaming Commission is one state-level starting point. There is no single nationwide online-casino licence; review the exact state, product, regulator, and operator.
  • France: ANJ — illegal online gambling offers. Online casino games are not among the generally authorised online gambling categories; do not present offshore online casinos as legal in France.
  • Germany: GGL — official whitelist of permitted providers. Match the exact domain, legal entity, and product scope.
  • Switzerland: Federal Gaming Board — online gambling. A foreign licence is not Swiss authorisation.
  • Great Britain: Gambling Commission business register. Verify the entity, active licence, relevant remote activity, and domain/trading name.
  • Netherlands: Kansspelautoriteit — register of licensees. Check the exact licensed entity and website/product.

Record what you verify

For each market, keep a short evidence record: regulator, applicable law or guidance, operator identity, permitted products, affiliate conditions, required disclosures, source links, date reviewed, and unresolved questions. Do not publish a market as “approved” until the evidence is current and reviewed.
