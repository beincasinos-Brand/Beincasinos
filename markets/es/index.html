Checking online gambling operators in Spain

Use Spain’s DGOJ operator search to match licensed operators, website domains and authorised products.

Editorial research guide · Updated 25 October 2026 · Recheck primary sources before relying on any market or operator claim.

Publication gate: SPANISH AUTHORISATION CHECK — verify the exact operator and domain.

Spain’s Dirección General de Ordenación del Juego (DGOJ) provides an official operator search with licence information and associated websites. A brand’s presence in another European market does not establish Spanish permission. Check the relevant operator entry and domain, and make sure the permitted product corresponds to the claim being made.

How to check a casino claim properly

  1. Match the exact domain. Record the customer-facing URL and redirects. A brand family can use different domains for different countries.
  2. Identify the legal operator. Read current terms and the legal footer. Compare the contracting entity with the regulator record; do not rely on a parent group name alone.
  3. Confirm the product permission. Casino games, poker, sports betting and virtual slots may have separate permissions. Never substitute one for another.
  4. Keep territory precise. State, province, canton or other local boundaries must remain visible. A permission in one place is not a universal licence.
  5. Read consumer terms. Check deposits and withdrawals, verification, fees, limits, complaints escalation, safer-gambling tools and full promotion terms.
  6. Record the evidence date. Registers, domains, licence statuses and rules can change. Recheck before publication and schedule a new review date.

Spain: common questions

Where should a Spanish operator be checked?

Use the DGOJ operator search and inspect the listed entity, associated website domains and relevant licence/product information.

Does a licence in another EU market establish Spanish permission?

No. Verify Spanish authorisation for the exact domain and product.

What if a brand name appears but the domain does not match?

Do not assume that the website is covered. Resolve the mismatch with current primary evidence before publishing a positive claim.

Questions to ask before trusting a listing

  • Does the official register identify this exact website or a clearly matching domain?
  • Does the listed entity match the entity named in current consumer terms?
  • Does the permission cover online casino games specifically?
  • Does the permission cover this territory and the current date?
  • Can readers find current withdrawal, complaints and safer-gambling terms?
  • Is the page clear about what has not been tested or verified?
