Online gambling rules, country by country

A licence is not a passport. The same brand can be legal in one country and not in another, so every review here is written for one market and one product.

Markets we review

Great Britain

Reviewed here

Online casinos need a Gambling Commission licence that covers remote casino.

What to check

  • The legal entity named in the terms matches the register.
  • The record includes remote casino, not only betting.
  • Your exact domain is listed against the operator.

Netherlands

Reviewed here

Online casino games need a permit from the Kansspelautoriteit (KSA).

What to check

  • The permit covers remote casino games.
  • The operator checks Cruks, the exclusion register.
  • Brand and domain match the permit holder.

Germany

Reviewed here

Online slots and some other casino products are legal only with a GGL licence.

What to check

  • The provider and domain are on the whitelist.
  • The permission covers the product you want (slots, poker and so on).
  • Limits and the OASIS exclusion system apply.

Switzerland

Reviewed here

Online casino games may be offered only by casinos with the right Swiss concession.

What to check

  • The brand is on the ESBK list of authorised online casinos.
  • The licensed casino named in the terms matches.
  • Foreign sites without Swiss authorisation can be blocked.

United States (Michigan)

Reviewed here

Michigan licenses internet casino operators for adults aged 21+ located in the state.

What to check

  • The operator is on the MGCB's licensed list.
  • You must be physically in Michigan.
  • A licence in another state does not apply here.

Australia

Guide only, no casino list

Online casino services for people in Australia are generally prohibited under the Interactive Gambling Act, so we do not list casinos for this market.

What to check

  • ACMA reports gambling-reform amendments passed Parliament on 19 August 2026, with most reforms due to start on 1 January 2027.
  • Offshore casino offers are not locally authorised.

France

Guide only, no casino list

The ANJ treats online casino games as not authorised. Sports betting and poker are separate categories with their own permissions, so we do not list casinos for this market.

What to check

  • Authorised online categories are not casino games.
  • Do not infer permission from a familiar brand name.

Spain

Research starting point

Remote casino games need a DGOJ licence for the specific game type.

What to check

  • The domain and legal entity match DGOJ records.
  • A sports-betting licence alone is not proof of casino permission.

Belgium

Research starting point

Online casinos need a Gaming Commission licence of the right class.

What to check

  • Check the licence class and the exact domain.
  • A land-based venue licence does not imply online permission.

Sweden

Research starting point

Online casinos need a Swedish Gambling Authority licence.

What to check

  • Check licence type, validity and the domain.
  • Enforcement history is published; read it.

What counts as evidence

  • Regulator record. The official register entry: legal entity, licence number, product scope and the date we checked.
  • Domain. The address you use must match the regulator record and the operator's legal notice. Similar names are not enough.
  • Product. Casino games, sports betting, poker and bingo are different permissions. One does not prove another.
  • Consumer terms. Bonus rules, deposits and withdrawals, privacy, safer-gambling tools and complaints.
  • Independent testing. We record what was actually tested and what was not. Operator marketing is never presented as a test.

Educational information, not legal advice. Laws, licences and domains change: check the regulator's own page before you rely on a claim.
