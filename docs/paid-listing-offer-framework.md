# Paid-listing offer framework — internal hypothesis only

**Status:** Draft for owner review. Not a public rate card, quote, revenue forecast, or claim of proven demand. No brand has agreed to pay. Do not send an offer until publisher identity, brand/name clearance, market scope, privacy/contact terms, audience evidence, and owner approval are in place.

## Commercial premise

A paid listing is a clearly disclosed advertising product, not a licence badge, safety endorsement, review score, or promise of ranking. Sell defined inventory and reporting—not trust, editorial outcomes, or guaranteed traffic. A new site with no measured audience should not imply that its placement has meaningful reach.

## Proposed test packages (prices are research hypotheses, not validated market rates)

| Package | Hypothesis price | Duration | Included deliverable | Conditions |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Sponsored directory card | EUR 100–250 | 30 days | One labelled sponsored card on a relevant market/category page; one end-of-period impression/click report if analytics are lawfully configured | Do not sell until the page has measured, reportable traffic and the market/product passes legal review |
| Sponsored category feature | EUR 250–600 | 30 days | Labelled feature module plus clearly separated standard editorial information; one report | No guaranteed impressions, clicks, leads, ranking, conversions, or search visibility |
| Multi-market campaign | EUR 600–1,500 | 30 days | Labelled inventory on up to three individually approved market pages and a consolidated report | Every market must separately pass licensing, advertising, affiliate and privacy review; never bundle illegal/restricted markets |
| Early-stage research interview | No charge | 20 minutes | Feedback on directory usability and the proposed media kit; no promised placement | Research only; not a paid listing or implied endorsement |

These ranges are starting hypotheses for willingness-to-pay interviews. Do not publish them as final prices. Validate with at least 10 relevant commercial conversations, record reasons for acceptance/refusal, compare like-for-like media kits, and adjust based on actual audience and fulfilment cost. A “yes” to an interview is not a “yes” to paying.

## Partnership models to validate (not yet offered)

The owner intends to explore more than fixed-fee listings. Treat the following as commercial hypotheses to validate with authorised operator contacts; do not publish rates or imply any current offer.

| Model | Possible commercial structure | Required controls |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed-fee listing | Fixed amount for a defined, labelled placement and period | Audience evidence, exact inventory, market/product approval, delivery/reporting definition, expiry and cancellation terms |
| CPA | Payment for a contractually defined qualifying action | Written definition of eligible action, attribution window, fraud/duplicate rules, validation, payout timing, market permission and disclosure |
| Revenue share | Contractual share of defined net revenue or other agreed base | Precise revenue base, deductions, negative carryover treatment if any, reporting, audit rights, attribution, payment currency/timing, termination and post-termination handling |
| Hybrid | Fixed fee plus CPA and/or revenue share | Separate fixed deliverables and performance calculation; avoid double-counting; define caps/floors if negotiated, reporting, attribution and termination |
| Sponsored content | Fixed fee for a clearly labelled educational/advertising asset | Strong ad label, editorial review, no disguised advertorial, no paid score/rank, claims substantiation and legal approval |

Do not assume every operator accepts all models. Some brands may only work through an existing affiliate network, and a network deal does not establish interest in buying a directory listing. Do not invent standard commission rates or use confidential third-party terms as market benchmarks without permission.

Before any performance-based deal, confirm that the attribution technology and reporting actually work, the privacy notice and consent design match the implementation, and the partner's programme terms allow the intended markets, pages, link placement, and promotion methods. Do not add tracking parameters, pixels, or cookies before the privacy and legal gate is approved.

## Inventory and measurement rules

- Label paid placements conspicuously as “Sponsored” or “Paid placement” next to the item, in plain language.
- Do not use paid status to influence independent editorial conclusions, licence checks, safety language, or organic ordering.
- Never fabricate impressions, clicks, users, geography, conversion rates, rankings, or expected revenue.
- Report the date range, analytics definition, bot filtering/limitations, and whether numbers are impressions or unique users.
- No tracking pixels, affiliate identifiers, non-essential cookies, or third-party audience sharing until the real implementation and privacy/consent obligations are reviewed.
- No paid placement goes live before a signed agreement, agreed creative/asset rights, approved market and product scope, payment terms, disclosure, expiry date, cancellation/refund terms, and re-review schedule are documented.
- Use rel="sponsored" for paid links where appropriate; do not sell followed links as an SEO benefit.
- Reject requests to remove material risk disclosures, disguise sponsorship, claim “safe” status, or target prohibited markets.

Use the internal [media-kit evidence template](./media-kit-evidence-template.md) to collect measured audience facts and limitations. Leave values as “Not measured” until actual dated reports exist; never create a media kit with invented traffic or projected results.

## Readiness gates before testing price

1. Legal publisher identity and monitored commercial contact confirmed.
2. Brand/name and domain risk professionally reviewed; public use approved by owner.
3. At least one market and product scope deliberately selected and reviewed.
4. Public site passes browser/mobile, accessibility, privacy, security and content review.
5. Actual analytics setup and privacy notice match; baseline report has a dated measurement window.
6. One-page media kit contains only measured facts and clear limitations.
7. Owner approves prices, deliverables, terms, refund/cancellation policy and outreach.
8. Prospect passes exact-domain/operator/licence/product/market checks; contact route comes from an official source.
9. Written commercial interest is recorded; no inference from an affiliate programme or supplier partnership.
10. Payment, signed terms and labelled placement are verified before marking a sale won.

## Discovery questions (no selling pressure)

- Who owns paid directory/media buying for this brand and market?
- Do you buy sponsored directory placements at all, or only affiliate/performance inventory?
- What minimum audience evidence and reporting do you require before considering a new publisher?
- Which markets and product categories are approved for your brand?
- What disclosures, link attributes, creative approvals, and contract terms do you require?
- If the project does not yet meet your audience threshold, what evidence would make a future review worthwhile?

Record answers accurately, respect no-contact requests, and do not repeatedly follow up after a decline. These questions are a research guide, not authorisation to contact anyone.
