## Article 1: How to check whether an online casino is licensed (5 countries)
Link to: /online-casinos-uk/ or /markets/

Licence badges prove nothing. The only reliable test is the regulator's own register, and it works the same way in every market: find the company, find the domain, check the product.

In Great Britain the Gambling Commission's public register lists operators and their licence account numbers, which must appear in the site footer. Match the legal entity in the terms and conditions to the register, then confirm the exact web address is listed. In the Netherlands the Kansspelautoriteit publishes licence holders; Dutch operators must also connect to Cruks, the exclusion register. In Germany the GGL publishes a whitelist of authorised providers, and virtual slots and online poker need a German permission. In Switzerland only casinos with a concession may offer online games, and the ESBK publishes the authorised list and blocks unlicensed foreign sites. In Michigan, operators must be licensed by the Michigan Gaming Control Board and players must be 21 or older and physically in the state.

The three-step test works anywhere: (1) company name from the terms, (2) search it on the register, (3) match the domain and product. If any step fails, do not deposit. Also be sceptical of "no verification" promises, mismatched company names, and licences from jurisdictions that do not cover your country.

A licence does not guarantee fast payouts or good bonuses. It means the operator is inside a system with rules, complaints routes and exclusion tools.

## Article 2: The real cost of a casino bonus: three offers, same headline
Link to: /tools/bonus-value-calculator/

Three casinos advertise "100% up to 100". Offer A has 20x wagering on the bonus. Offer B has 35x on the bonus. Offer C has 35x on deposit plus bonus. The headline is identical.

Required turnover is 2,000, 3,500 and 7,000. If the game you play returns 96% on average, the theoretical cost of wagering is 4% of the turnover: 80, 140 and 280. So Offer A gives a theoretical positive value of 20, Offer B a loss of 40, and Offer C a loss of 180, before counting maximum-bet limits, expiry and cashout caps.

None of this predicts what happens in one session, which can end far above or below the average. The point is how to compare: ask what the multiple applies to, calculate the turnover, subtract the average cost, then look at the caps. If you cannot answer the questions from the terms, skip the offer. Bonuses are optional.

## Article 3: What self-exclusion covers (and what it does not)
Link to: /guides/online-gambling-self-exclusion/

National exclusion schemes block you from many licensed operators at once: GAMSTOP in Great Britain, Cruks in the Netherlands, OASIS in Germany, and BetStop in Australia for licensed wagering. They are free, you choose the period, and most cannot be shortened once started.

They only work where operators are connected. Unlicensed sites are outside every scheme, which is why searching for alternatives defeats the purpose. Combine exclusion with blocking software, removing saved cards, unsubscribing from marketing and telling someone. If gambling is harming you, contact a free support service, such as BeGambleAware or the National Gambling Helpline in Great Britain or Gambling Help Online in Australia.

Sources to cite: GAMSTOP, Cruks, GGL, BetStop, ACMA, BeGambleAware.
