# Six-month audience and measurement plan

This is a planning framework, not a traffic or revenue forecast. Search visibility, traffic, brand responses, and paid-listing demand are uncertain. Review evidence monthly and change direction when the data says to.

## Operating principles

- Build for readers first: answer a real question, cite primary sources, explain limits, and update dated facts.
- Publish only reviewed pages. Do not create 100 thin casino pages to meet the prospect target.
- Keep the 100-brand list private/internal until each profile passes the evidence and market checks in `prelaunch-launch-checklist.md`.
- Do not add analytics, advertising pixels, affiliate tracking, or non-essential cookies until the privacy notice and any required consent controls match the actual setup.
- Do not promise brands impressions, clicks, conversions, rankings, or audience size without measured evidence.
- Paid listings must be clearly labelled and must not influence editorial conclusions.
- Keep the prelaunch crawl block in place until the owner explicitly approves launch and indexing.

## Month-by-month work plan

### Month 1 — Clear the foundation
- Resolve the name/trademark question before public launch or further branding investment. The domain purchase is owner-reported complete; this is not trademark clearance.
- Confirm the first market for actual publication, publisher identity, monitored contact route, and a host whose terms permit the intended commercial model. The broader research scope is AU, US, FR, DE, CH, GB, and NL; it is not permission to promote offers in all of them.
- Review current pages for usefulness, factual sourcing, internal links, and responsible-gambling language.
- Define a baseline spreadsheet for content inventory and monthly metrics; record zero/unknown honestly rather than estimating.
- Do not launch publicly while the release checklist has unresolved launch blockers.

**Evidence to retain:** name-review decision, market decision, content inventory, hosting terms and cost approval, dated release checklist.

### Month 2 — Prepare a small, useful launch set
- Prioritize evergreen explainers and tools that solve specific reader problems.
- Review titles, descriptions, headings, internal links, mobile navigation, keyboard use, and tool edge cases.
- Conduct real desktop/mobile browser checks and manual accessibility review; record issues and retest evidence.
- Finalize privacy, contact, editorial, correction, commercial-disclosure, and safer-gambling information to match the actual product.
- Keep unsupported market-specific claims and unverified operator profiles unpublished.

**Evidence to retain:** reviewed page list, source links, browser test notes, resolved defect list, approved release commit.

### Month 3 — Launch only after approval
- Complete every applicable release gate. Verify the existing domain registration; get explicit owner approval before paid hosting, deployment, DNS changes, or enabling indexing.
- Configure the real domain, HTTPS, canonical/social URLs, robots rules, and sitemap.
- Verify the domain in Google Search Console and submit the real sitemap only after indexing is approved.
- Inspect indexing coverage, crawl errors, mobile usability signals where available, and search queries; do not interpret early volatility as a trend.
- Publish at a sustainable pace, prioritizing quality over page count.

**Evidence to retain:** approval record, production URLs, Search Console verification date, submitted sitemap, release commit, launch date.

### Month 4 — Improve based on reader intent
- Review queries and pages with impressions but weak click-through; improve titles/snippets only when the page genuinely answers that intent.
- Review pages receiving impressions but little engagement; improve clarity, source citations, examples, and navigation rather than adding filler.
- Fix broken links, stale facts, and pages excluded from indexing for a valid reason.
- Start no outbound sales pitch based on imagined audience numbers; prepare only a factual internal media-kit outline.

### Month 5 — Validate trust and operational quality
- Recheck time-sensitive claims and official regulator/operator sources on a documented cadence.
- Review corrections, complaints, privacy requests, and contact-response time.
- Review whether the site is attracting the intended geography and whether the published content fits the applicable rules.
- Audit all commercial links and disclosures if any have been approved; otherwise keep them absent.
- If traffic is still small, continue improving reader value rather than selling unsupported reach.

### Month 6 — Decide from evidence, not the calendar
- Compare the last 28 days with the prior 28 days and, where available, the same seasonal period; note small samples and volatility.
- Review organic impressions, clicks, click-through rate, indexed useful pages, top queries, country mix, referral quality, and user feedback.
- Decide whether the audience evidence is sufficient to test a clearly labelled paid-listing offer. There is no automatic threshold and no guarantee of sales.
- Build any external media kit only from dated, reproducible measurements and state the measurement window, source, and limitations.
- Continue to defer affiliate links until programme terms, approved territories, tracking, privacy, and disclosures have been reviewed.

## Monthly measurement sheet

Record one row per month. Prefer Google Search Console for organic search performance. Add analytics only if it is necessary, lawfully configured, and disclosed.

| Metric | How to record it | Interpretation guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Search impressions | Search Console, date range and property noted | Visibility, not proof of visits or demand |
| Organic clicks | Search Console, same date range | Clicks are not unique people or conversions |
| Search CTR | Clicks divided by impressions; report the source range | Compare pages/query groups carefully; positions and intent differ |
| Average position | Search Console, note filters | Directional aggregate, not a guaranteed ranking |
| Useful pages indexed | Sample/URL inspection and coverage data | Indexing is not a ranking or quality endorsement |
| Top queries and pages | Export top entries with date range | Use to improve answers; avoid chasing irrelevant queries |
| Country/market mix | Search Console geography where available | Do not assume legal availability from visitor location |
| Corrections and stale claims | Count and summarize logged cases | Trust and accuracy matter more than volume |
| Contact enquiries | Count legitimate enquiries, avoid unnecessary personal data | Enquiry count alone does not prove willingness to pay |
| Brand outreach | Only after audience evidence and approval; record stage in sales CSV | Replies and proposals are not revenue |
| Paid listings | Signed agreement and payment status, kept separate from editorial scoring | Never count a verbal interest as revenue |

## Commercial readiness review

Before offering paid placements, write down:
- Measurement window and exact data source.
- Actual impressions/clicks and relevant audience geography, with limitations.
- Number of useful, reviewed pages and update process.
- Exact placement, duration, renewal/cancellation and refund terms.
- What is included and excluded; no promise of rankings, traffic, conversions, legal status, safety, or a positive editorial verdict.
- Clear paid-placement labelling and separation from editorial scoring.
- Contact handling, invoicing, privacy, and complaint/correction process.

If the evidence is too small or unstable, say so and postpone the offer. Six months is a review checkpoint, not a monetization deadline.
