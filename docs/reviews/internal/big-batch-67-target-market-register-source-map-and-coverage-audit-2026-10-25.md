## Primary-source register map and release implications

### Australia (AU) — online casino acquisition excluded pending qualified legal review
- ACMA explains that online casinos are prohibited services under the Interactive Gambling Act and must not be advertised in Australia: https://www.acma.gov.au/interactivegambling
- ACMA's register is for licensed interactive gambling providers, not proof that online casino games are permitted: https://www.acma.gov.au/check-if-gambling-operator-legal
- Action: do not create Australia-targeted online-casino affiliate acquisition pages from international licences. Keep the existing AU publication block.

### Canada (CA) — province-specific only; Ontario is not nationwide Canada
- iGaming Ontario's regulated-site directory says it lists operators/sites contracted and regulated for Ontario and was accurate as of 1 September 2026: https://igamingontario.ca/en/operator/operators
- Action: candidate work must specify province, regulator, exact site and permitted product. Keep aggregate CA publication blocked; no Ontario evidence can be represented as nationwide authorisation.

### Great Britain (GB) — exact UKGC business, domain and activity checks
- UK Gambling Commission public register supports searching by business, trading name and domain and distinguishes licence status: https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/public-register/businesses
- Remote sector guidance explains the licence requirement for serving GB consumers and the register's domain/activity fields: https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/licensees-and-businesses/sectors/sector/remote/
- Action: preserve exact domain, legal account holder, current status and remote casino activity; a betting-only permission or white-label/domain listing alone is not proof of an active casino product.

### United States (US) — exact state-level authorisation required
- This batch does not substitute a single nationwide register. Continue using the official regulator and authorised-platform sources already documented for Pennsylvania, New Jersey and Michigan.
- Action: do not publish aggregate US pages or infer state availability from a brand's national/international presence. Each row must name the state and match the exact domain, legal operator and casino product.

### Switzerland (CH) — ESBK official online-platform list
- ESBK lists ten online casino platforms and states that a licence extension plus permits for individual games are required: https://www.esbk.admin.ch/en/online-gambling/
- ESBK's unauthorised-platform page links the current block list: https://www.esbk.admin.ch/en/unauthorised-online-games
- Action: compare exact brand/domain and operator to the official list; an image, affiliate listing, or generic casino licence is not enough. The two current candidate rows are not approved merely because their brands appear on the regulator list.

### Germany (DE) — whitelist scope and product distinctions
- GGL whitelist (shown as updated 2 October 2026) identifies permitted gambling offers and says it is updated at least monthly: https://www.gluecksspiel-behoerde.de/de/fuer-spielende/uebersicht-erlaubter-anbieter-whitelist
- GGL explains that online casino games fall under state-level permissions and are not all authorised by the GGL itself: https://www.gluecksspiel-behoerde.de/de/fuer-spielende/informationen-fuer-spielende-faqs/faq-was-sind-legale-online-casinos-in-deutschland
- Action: record game type and territory separately. Virtual slots, poker, sports betting and state-limited online casino permissions are not interchangeable.

### France (FR) — online casino acquisition excluded

- Action: match the exact domain, operator, licence class and current expiry/status. Foreign licences do not establish Belgian authorisation; keep the Belgian publication gate.

## Shared minimum evidence package for every candidate
1. Official regulator record with exact domain, legal entity, licence/product scope, status and access date.
2. First-party terms, privacy/cookie policy, safer-gambling tools, complaints/ADR, deposit/withdrawal methods, fees/limits and payment processing rules.
3. Current dated bonus terms captured from the official offer page; no amount or wagering claim without the full applicable conditions.
4. Consumer journey test and withdrawal test status accurately stated; never imply a test occurred if it did not.
5. Image source and documented reuse rights; a publicly accessible logo is not automatically reusable.
6. Completed score rubric, evidence bundle, separate primary and secondary reviewer, independent review date, affiliate-disclosure review and editorial approval.
7. Mobile/accessibility checks and market-specific legal approval where required.
