# BeInCasinos commercial partnership operating playbook

**Status:** Internal strategy and process. Not a public rate card, legal opinion, or permission to sell. No operator is assumed to have agreed to pay. Use only after the owner has cleared the launch, privacy, market, and audience gates.

## 1. Positioning

BeInCasinos should sell clearly defined advertising inventory and transparent reporting—not editorial influence, a licence badge, guaranteed traffic, or guaranteed commercial results. Editorial eligibility and review outcomes remain independent of commercial status.

The initial objective is to learn which operators have a legitimate reason to test a new directory and what evidence their procurement teams require. The first commercial milestone is qualified feedback, not a fabricated revenue target.

## 2. Commercial models to test

| Model | What the buyer pays for | Evidence and contract items required |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed-fee listing | A specific, clearly labelled directory placement for a fixed term | Exact page/position, start/end dates, market eligibility, label, link treatment, update schedule, cancellation/refund, invoice and report |
| CPA | A contractually defined qualifying action | Event definition, attribution window, duplicate/fraud rules, validation, payout timing, allowed markets, disclosures and privacy controls |
| Revenue share | A defined share of an agreed revenue base | Net/gross base, deductions, attribution, reporting, audit rights, payment currency/timing, negative carryover if any, termination and post-termination handling |
| Hybrid | Fixed inventory fee plus CPA and/or revenue share | Separately itemized deliverables and calculations; attribution and double-counting rules; caps/floors only if negotiated; all terms in writing |
| Sponsored content | A specific content deliverable that is visibly identified as sponsored | Editorial separation, claim substantiation, market review, approval boundaries, correction rights, disclosure placement, term and removal rules |

Do not assume that an operator having an affiliate programme means it buys paid listings. A contact agreeing to an interview is not buyer intent; buyer intent is not a signed agreement; a signed agreement is not paid revenue until payment is recorded.

## 3. Buyer qualification

Before a prospect can be called qualified, record:

- Exact brand, official domain, legal entity, product, and relevant market.
- Current primary-source regulator/domain evidence where applicable.
- Official contact page or partnership route and the date verified.
- Any unresolved licensing, white-label, ownership, enforcement, or market-scope issue.
- The specific audience or directory need the product could serve.
- Commercial stage with evidence: researched, contact verified, draft ready, contacted, replied, qualified buyer, proposal sent, signed, paid, declined, or do-not-contact.
- Last contact date, next permitted action, and opt-out/complaint state.

Do not infer contact names, emails, launch dates, budgets, willingness to pay, or operator approval. Record uncertainty explicitly and keep unqualified prospects on hold.

## 4. Pricing and pilot discipline

Internal pricing documents contain hypotheses only. Do not publish or quote a rate until inventory and the evidence behind it are reviewed. A defensible proposal should specify the page and placement, market, duration, label, link attributes, update cadence, what reporting is available, measurement definitions, price/currency/tax, cancellation/refund, renewal, and start/end dates.

For a new site without measured audience, avoid implying that a listing will deliver meaningful reach. If a research interview or no-charge usability pilot is used, state in writing that it does not promise a paid placement, endorsement, score, ranking, or future partnership.

Validate pricing using comparable, documented conversations and actual objections. Keep counts separate for no response, not interested, interested in a proposal, proposal requested, negotiation, signed, invoiced, and paid. Never turn a forecast or a prospect list into a revenue claim.

## 5. Non-negotiable editorial controls

- Payment never determines eligibility, licensing conclusions, editorial score, safety language, or ranking.
- Clearly label sponsored placements beside the placement itself on desktop and mobile.
- Never sell “verified”, “safe”, “trusted”, “best”, or “top-ranked” status.
- Do not hide a paid placement inside an organic editorial list.
- Do not link to an operator for a market unless its product and market permission have been checked for the intended claim and audience.
- Re-check operator/domain status at publication and on a scheduled cadence; pause or remove a listing when evidence changes or a credible issue is unresolved.
- Provide a correction, complaint, and removal path with a monitored owner.

## 6. Outreach safety and privacy

Only use official, relevant business contact channels. Check local direct-marketing, anti-spam, privacy, and platform rules before outreach. Do not scrape personal contact details, guess addresses, send mass unsolicited messages, or keep contacting opt-outs. Personalize a relevant message, identify the publisher accurately, explain the reason for contact, and provide a simple way to decline further contact.

Keep only necessary business-contact information, document its source and collection date, restrict access, and define a retention/deletion period. Do not place private prospect information in public pages or build artifacts.

## 7. First 90 days after launch approval

### Days 1–15: verify and instrument
- Confirm live routes, disclosure and privacy notices, and domain/HTTPS behaviour.
- Enable only approved measurement tools and consent controls.
- Verify Search Console ownership and record a baseline without promising outcomes.
- Inspect page indexing and fix technical errors; do not bulk-publish unreviewed profiles.

### Days 16–30: learn from users
- Improve the highest-value educational guides and tools based on observed queries and usability feedback.
- Review search impressions, clicks, landing pages, country/device mix, and query intent using dated first-party reports.
- Conduct small, compliant discovery interviews only if the owner approves and official contacts are verified.

### Days 31–60: qualify inventory
- Select a small group of candidates with verified official domains, operator identity, market scope, and plausible fit.
- Define one or two exact, clearly labelled placements and a transparent report.
- Ask what procurement evidence is required; do not present speculative traffic as fact.
- Record objections and whether the contact asked for a proposal.

### Days 61–90: consider a controlled pilot
- Only if audience evidence, market/legal review, privacy controls, and offer terms are ready, issue a limited written proposal.
- Start no placement before the agreement and disclosures are complete.
- Report only measured delivery, including limitations; record cancellation and complaint handling.
- Review whether the pilot improved reader utility without compromising editorial independence.

These are sequencing suggestions, not promises that traffic, sales, or partnerships will occur within 90 days.

## 8. Monthly owner dashboard

Track actual values with source, period, and definitions:

- Organic clicks and impressions by market/page/query.
- Indexed canonical pages and technical errors.
- Reader engagement metrics only where lawfully measured.
- Number of profiles passing evidence gates; number on hold; reasons for hold.
- Qualified commercial contacts, replies, proposal requests, signed contracts, and paid invoices as separate counts.
- Sponsored inventory live, disclosure checks, corrections, complaints, removals, and opt-outs.
- Privacy/security issues, stale-source checks, and unresolved launch risks.

Write “not measured” when data is unavailable. Never fill a blank with a guess.
