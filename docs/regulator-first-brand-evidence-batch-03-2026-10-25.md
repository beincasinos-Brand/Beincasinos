# Regulator-first market evidence batch 03 — 25 October 2026

**Scope:** Official-registry research to improve the BeInCasinos review pipeline. This is an internal research note, not a consumer-facing endorsement. A listed site is not automatically legal in every region, nor does a regulator listing prove payout quality, bonus fairness, or customer service.

## Current primary-source findings

### Great Britain (GB)
- Official source: UK Gambling Commission public register, https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/public-register/businesses/
- The register page states it can be searched by business name, trading name, domain or account number, and that the register was last updated **25 October 2026** at the time checked.
- The register explains that licence status includes Active and Expired; domain/trading-name data is supplied by the gambling business and the Commission cannot guarantee third-party-provided information.
- **Required profile evidence:** exact domain + legal account name + active licence status + relevant remote casino activity. Save a dated register result; do not infer that a similarly named company or affiliate site is licensed.

### Canada (CA): separate province-level gates
- Ontario's official regulated-market directory: https://www.igamingontario.ca/en/player/regulated-igaming-market
- The directory reports **49 operators and 84 gaming websites**, and says its list was accurate as of **1 September 2026**. It includes product categories, so verify the exact site and category rather than relying on brand name alone.
- British Columbia's official gambling regulator explains that PlayNow.com is the only regulated online gambling website permitted in BC: https://www.igcobc.ca/commercial-gambling
- **Required profile evidence:** province named on the review; exact website and operator in that province's official source; product category; local eligibility. An Ontario listing must not be presented as Canadian-wide authorisation, and an offshore licence is not a substitute for provincial permission.

### Australia (AU): do not conflate wagering with online casino
- Official ACMA source: https://www.acma.gov.au/check-if-gambling-operator-legal
- The register is explicitly for licensed interactive gambling providers/wagering services. Its page warns consumers to check the register before using an online wagering service.
- **Required profile evidence:** identify whether the product is wagering, lottery, or casino-style online gaming; check the relevant Australian law and regulator scope. A listed wagering service does **not** establish that online casino slots or table games are authorised. Hold casino-style claims unless product-specific legal eligibility is independently established.

### Netherlands (NL)
- Official live licence register: https://kansspelautoriteit.nl/register-licensees
- KSA says users can search company name, website, app or licence number and recommends checking the register before registering.
- **Required profile evidence:** exact website/domain and operator in the live register on the day of review. A licence announcement or brand family name is not a substitute for matching the exact consumer domain.

### United States (US)
- There is no single national online-casino licence that can support a US-wide claim. Use the relevant state regulator and the exact state/product.
- A state regulator directory can help identify the appropriate authority: North American Gaming Regulators Association, https://www.nagra.org/State-and-Province-Gaming-Regulatory-Agencies
- **Required profile evidence:** state, regulator, legal operator, approved domain, and product category. Never use one state's licence to label a site “licensed in the USA.”

## Editorial scoring and publication gate
Keep the existing 100-point methodology:
- Terms clarity/fairness 25
- Payments and withdrawal transparency 20
- Safer-gambling controls 20
- Complaints/support 15
- Operator/licence transparency 10
- Usability/accessibility 10

Do not publish a numeric score or star rating until the market/product eligibility gate passes and each weighted criterion has dated evidence. Do not invent bonus amounts, payment speeds, player testing, support tests, RTP claims, or logos. If evidence is incomplete, display “Not rated — verification incomplete” and state what is missing.

## Public-site and SEO implications
- This batch is **internal research only** and does not change public review copy, robots.txt, sitemap, or deployment.
- The existing review pages should not be bulk-marked as “verified” based on generic operator terms. Official register match, current terms, and consumer-product scope are separate checks.
- Prioritise accurate coverage across the 12 target markets over adding more near-duplicate pages in a single market. The current distribution is heavily concentrated in Spain and lacks Australia/France coverage; that gap should be corrected only after legal/product gates, not by copying generic text.
- For search quality, each published review needs original, useful comparison and transparent evidence/methodology; mass-produced thin reviews should not be treated as a shortcut to rankings.
