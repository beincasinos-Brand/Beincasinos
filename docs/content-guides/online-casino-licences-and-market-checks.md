# Online Casino Licences: How to Check a Brand for Your Market

Gambling rules vary by country, state, province and product. This is an evidence-checking framework, not legal advice. A licence in one place does not automatically authorise a product everywhere.

## Brand name is not proof

A brand may use different legal entities, domains and products in different markets. A regulator record may cover one domain or one type of gambling but not another. Before treating a service as eligible, identify the exact website domain, legal operator named in the terms, licence holder and reference where applicable, official regulator record and current status, product category covered, geographic limits, and date checked.

If these cannot be reconciled, the editorial status is HOLD — ELIGIBILITY NOT VERIFIED.

## Primary-source workflow

1. Find the relevant regulator's official public register, not just a badge on the operator's website.
2. Search the exact company name and, where available, exact domain and licence number.
3. Check current status, permitted activities and restrictions.
4. Compare the legal entity and domain against the site's current terms.
5. Check official warnings, sanctions or public notices relevant to the entity/domain.
6. Save the source URL, date checked, reviewer and a short evidence note.
7. Recheck before publication and during scheduled updates.

A screenshot can document what was seen but does not replace checking the current official record.

## Product permissions matter

Online casino games, poker, sports betting, lotteries and land-based casino operations may be subject to different rules. A company authorised for one product may not be authorised for another. A group company's licence does not prove that every sister brand or domain is covered.

## Market-specific caution

- **Australia (AU):** Do not assume an offshore online casino is lawful for Australian residents because it accepts Australian dollars or advertises locally. Check current official Australian regulatory information and product restrictions.
- **United States (US):** Online casino permissions are state-specific. Verify the state, operator, product and authorised domain against that state's official regulator.
- **Canada (CA):** The position varies by province and product. Verify the specific provincial authorisation and domain; do not generalise an Ontario status to all of Canada.
- **Great Britain (GB):** Check the UK Gambling Commission's official register for the legal entity, domain and relevant activities. Do not confuse Great Britain with the whole United Kingdom.
- **Switzerland (CH):** Verify the relevant Swiss official list and specific authorisation/domain.
- **Germany (DE):** Check current official German regulator information and permitted product category. A foreign licence alone is not evidence of German authorisation.
- **Netherlands (NL):** Check the Dutch regulator's official licensee information and exact domain/product.
- **Sweden (SE):** Check the Swedish regulator's official register and relevant licence category, operator and domain.
- **Spain (ES):** Check DGOJ official operator information for the company, domain and authorised game categories. A land-based venue's name or a foreign group licence does not establish online permission.
- **Italy (IT):** Check the competent Italian authority's official concession/operator information and exact domain/product.
- **France (FR):** Do not imply that a standard online casino games licence exists for France. Verify current official French rules and distinguish online poker or betting from casino-style games.
- **Belgium (BE):** Check the Belgian Gaming Commission's official lists, relevant licence category and exact domain.

These are screening cautions, not a complete legal analysis. Rules can change, so source each market statement at publication time.

## Editorial statuses

- **ELIGIBLE FOR REVIEW:** Exact operator/domain/product permission is supported by a current primary source.
- **HOLD:** A key detail is missing, ambiguous, outdated or contradictory.
- **EXCLUDE:** Official evidence shows the operator/product is not eligible for the intended market.
- **NOT RATED:** Eligibility may be known, but evidence is insufficient for a defensible quality score.

Never use a numerical quality score to override an eligibility hold or exclusion.

## Common mistakes

- Trusting a logo or licence number without checking the regulator.
- Using an affiliate landing page as the only evidence.
- Treating a parent company's authorisation as proof for every brand.
- Treating a redirect as proof that the destination is authorised.
- Reusing an old verification date after a company, domain or law changes.
- Saying “legal” without naming the market, product and source.
- Publishing a bonus or payment claim before checking market-specific terms.

Before signing up, check official regulator information for your location, read current terms and confirm the exact domain. If the position is unclear, do not rely on a review site's summary as a substitute for current official information.
