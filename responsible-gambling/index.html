Wellbeing comes first

It’s okay not to gamble.

Gambling can lead to financial loss and harm. There is no guaranteed way to win, and gambling should never be treated as a source of income.

Notice the warning signs

Consider taking a break if you are spending more than intended, chasing losses, hiding activity, borrowing to gamble, or feeling anxious about money or gambling. You do not need to wait for a crisis to seek support.

Steps that may create space

  • Stop and take time away from gambling services.
  • Use account time-outs, closure, or self-exclusion tools where available.
  • Consider bank transaction blocks or device blocking tools if offered locally.
  • Tell someone you trust and seek help from a qualified support service.
  • Do not borrow money or chase losses to try to recover previous spending.

Find support where you live

Look for official public-health resources or a recognised local support organisation. In Great Britain, GamCare provides information and support. In other countries, use a local, verified service; the correct options vary by location.

Support in Great Britain

For readers in Great Britain, the GamCare website provides information about support options. Service availability and eligibility can change; check its current official guidance. Readers elsewhere should seek a verified local health service or recognised support organisation rather than assume a UK service covers them.

Urgent support

If you are in immediate danger or may harm yourself, contact local emergency services or a crisis line in your country and reach out to a trusted person now.

18+ where applicable. Age limits and gambling laws vary. If gambling is not legal or available to you where you live, do not access it.
